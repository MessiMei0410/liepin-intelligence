# Recruiter Methodology Reference

Use this reference only when a headhunting request needs recruiter process design, not for every routine routing task.

## Intake Questions

Ask the smallest useful set:

1. What business problem does this hire solve?
2. Which requirements are non-negotiable, and which are trainable?
3. What backgrounds have succeeded or failed before?
4. Which target companies, teams, or product lines matter most?
5. What compensation range, location, travel, and reporting constraints exist?
6. What is the interview process and who can make the decision?
7. Why would a strong candidate leave their current role for this one?

Convert answers into hard gates, preference criteria, sell points, objections, and search keywords.

## Candidate Scorecard

Use a 4-part scorecard:

| Dimension | What to verify |
| --- | --- |
| Hard requirements | Education, years, industry, certifications, tools, required domain |
| Role capability | Scope handled, complexity, ownership, measurable outcomes |
| Motivation and closeability | reason for move, preferred city, compensation, family constraints, competing offers |
| Risk | stability, non-compete, notice period, salary evidence gaps, mismatch with client culture |

Use evidence labels:

- Strong evidence: resume line, project result, number, named tool/company/process
- Medium evidence: plausible role responsibility, partial match, interview claim
- Weak evidence: generic wording, inferred background, unverified statement

## Search Strategy

Build searches from four layers:

1. Role titles: exact titles, adjacent titles, seniority variants.
2. Domain keywords: product/process/equipment/tool names, certifications, project vocabulary.
3. Target companies: direct competitors, suppliers/customers, adjacent industries, regional clusters.
4. Exclusion terms: noisy industries, unrelated meanings, junior/sales/admin variants.

For Boolean-style search, group terms as:

```text
(core role OR adjacent title) +(domain keyword OR tool/process) +(target company OR industry) -noise
```

For Chinese sourcing, include full company names, short names, English aliases, historical names, product names, and common typo/spacing variants.

## Outreach Message

Keep first contact short:

- identity and reason for reaching out
- one-line role hook
- why the candidate seems relevant
- low-pressure ask for a brief call
- no exaggerated compensation or unsupported claims

Avoid disclosing confidential client details unless the user confirms it is allowed.

## Interview Plan

A useful interview plan includes:

- capability to verify
- concrete question
- what a strong answer sounds like
- red flags
- follow-up probe

Favor behavioral and evidence-based questions: "Tell me about the most comparable project" beats "Are you familiar with X?"

## Pipeline Review

When asked to review a project, summarize:

- sourced count, screened count, recommended count, interview count, offer count
- conversion bottleneck
- recurring rejection reasons
- target companies producing viable profiles
- next search expansion or narrowing action

Look for stage-specific failure:

- too few sourced candidates: target pool or keywords are wrong
- many sourced, few qualified: hard gates too hidden or keyword noise too high
- many qualified, few interested: sell points, compensation, location, or timing problem
- many interviews, no offer: scorecard/client calibration problem

## Compliance Guardrails

Use only job-related criteria. Do not rank, exclude, or target candidates by protected attributes such as age, gender, marital status, family status, ethnicity, religion, disability, pregnancy, or similar traits.

For sensitive constraints, reframe to job requirements:

- "must travel 50%" instead of family assumptions
- "must be onsite in Suzhou 5 days/week" instead of marital/family screening
- "requires Mandarin-English technical communication" instead of nationality

If user notes include sensitive attributes, preserve only what is necessary for lawful job-related logistics and avoid using it as a selection factor.
